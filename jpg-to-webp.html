Should you convert JPEG photos to WebP?

WebP is useful for websites that support it, but re-encoding an already compressed JPEG can introduce additional artifacts. Keep the original JPG as your source file.

Start at 85% quality, inspect faces, gradients and textures, then adjust as needed. The same percentage in JPEG and WebP does not mean the same quality or file size.

Avoid unnecessary repeated conversion

Repeated lossy conversion can accumulate damage. Export once from the best available original and retain a source copy. If the new file is larger, changing format alone has not helped your storage or bandwidth.

Frequently asked questions

Are JPG and JPEG different formats?

No. JPG and JPEG are filename extensions for the same image format; both are accepted here.

Can WebP recover detail lost in JPG?

No. Converting cannot recover detail already discarded by JPEG compression. A new lossy encoding may introduce further changes.

Will photo metadata be preserved?

Source EXIF, GPS and other metadata are not deliberately copied. Browser decoding and encoding can change color handling; inspect important photos and retain originals.
