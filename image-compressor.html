    How to compress an image

    1. Choose your images, or drag them onto the box above. You can add up to 50 at once.
    2. Leave the output on Auto and set the quality. 75 is the default for compression.
    3. Click Compress Image and compare the sizes shown.
    4. Download one file, or all files together as a ZIP.

    How the compressor works

    The tool re-encodes each image at the quality you select. In Auto mode, JPG files stay JPG, so they work everywhere. PNG and WebP files are saved as WebP, because a browser cannot make a PNG smaller without discarding detail, and WebP is usually much smaller. If you need a different format, change the Output setting.

    Choosing a quality

    QualityTypical use
    85 to 90Photos you want to keep nearly identical

    50 to 65Thumbnails and previews where small size matters most

    Size matters more than quality

    An image that is 4000 pixels wide but shown at 800 pixels wastes most of its data. Resize images to the largest size they are displayed at, then compress them. Also set width and height on your image tags so the page does not jump while it loads. The format guide has an example.

    Frequently asked questions

    Which formats does the compressor create?

    In Auto mode JPG files stay JPG, and PNG and WebP files become WebP. You can also force WebP or JPG.

    Why did my image get bigger?

    Some files are already well optimized, and a different setting can then produce a larger file. The tool tells you when this happens. Lower the quality or keep the original.

    Does compression reduce quality?

    Yes, slightly. The quality slider controls the trade-off. Values from 75 to 85 are usually hard to tell from the original on screen.

    Are my files uploaded?

    No. Compression happens inside your browser, and the files are never sent to a server.
