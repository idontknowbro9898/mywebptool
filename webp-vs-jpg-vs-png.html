WebP vs JPG vs PNG: Which Image Format Should You Use?

A practical comparison of the three image formats you meet most often, with clear rules for choosing between them.

The short answer

  • Use WebP for images on websites. It gives the smallest files for both photos and graphics, and every current major browser supports it.
  • Use JPG when you need a photo that opens everywhere, such as email attachments and upload forms.
  • Use PNG for logos, screenshots and images you will keep editing, or when you need lossless quality with transparency.

How much smaller is WebP?

Google's WebP documentation reports that lossless WebP images are about 26% smaller than PNG images, and that lossy WebP images are 25% to 34% smaller than JPEG images at equivalent quality. The real saving varies from image to image, so measure your own files with the JPG to WebP or PNG to WebP converter, which shows the result before you download.

Which format for which job?

Photos on a website

Use WebP at a quality of 75 to 85. Keep a JPG as a fallback if you must support very old software.

Logos and icons

Use SVG when you have a vector version. Otherwise use PNG, or WebP at a high quality (90 or above) if you want a smaller file.

Screenshots

PNG keeps text sharp. WebP at quality 90 or higher is usually smaller and still looks clean.

Images you will edit again

Keep a PNG or the original source file. Every time a lossy file is saved again, a little more detail is lost.

Email and upload forms

Use JPG or PNG. Many forms do not accept WebP. Use the WebP to JPG or WebP to PNG converter.

How to add WebP to your website safely

The picture element lets the browser pick WebP when it can and fall back to JPG otherwise. Always set width and height so the page does not shift while images load.

<picture>
  <source srcset="photo.webp" type="image/webp">
  <img src="photo.jpg" alt="Describe the image" width="1200" height="800" loading="lazy">
</picture>

Do not lazy-load the main image at the top of the page. It is often the largest element on screen, and loading it late slows down the page. Resize images to the size they are shown at before compressing them. The image compressor can help with the last step.

Frequently asked questions

Is WebP better than JPG?

For websites, usually yes, because the files are smaller at similar quality. For email and upload forms, JPG is safer because more software accepts it.

Can WebP replace PNG?

For most web uses, yes. WebP supports transparency and can be lossless. Keep PNG when you need the widest support or will keep editing the file.

Does using WebP improve SEO?

Not directly. Smaller images help pages load faster, and page experience is one of many things search engines consider. Good content and relevant images matter far more.
