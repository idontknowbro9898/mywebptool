Choose how to reduce file size

JPG and WebP: lowering quality usually reduces bytes but can introduce visible artifacts. Quality values are encoder settings, not percentages of the original quality.

PNG: the browser does not expose adjustable PNG compression through the quality slider. Keep PNG for lossless encoding of the decoded pixels, reduce dimensions if suitable, or choose WebP for lossy output with transparency.

How to compress an image

  1. Choose PNG, JPG or WebP files. Keep input format processes each image in its own format.
  2. Adjust quality for JPG or WebP. Optionally enter a smaller maximum width.
  3. Convert and check the byte count and percentage change. Download the results you want to use.

Why compression sometimes makes files larger

Your original may already be optimized by a different encoder. Re-encoding it with browser defaults can increase size. There is no fixed reduction percentage or target-byte guarantee.

Optimize images for their actual display size

Resizing a large photo to match its intended use can save more than changing format alone. Keep a high-resolution original for print or future edits. Search visibility depends on many factors; image compression alone cannot ensure ranking improvements.

Frequently asked questions

Can I compress an image to an exact size such as 100 KB?

This tool does not target an exact byte size. Lower JPG or WebP quality or reduce maximum width, convert again and check the resulting size.

Does the quality slider compress PNG?

No. Browser PNG export ignores that setting. Resize the PNG or choose a different output format if appropriate.

Does keep input format preserve the original file?

It keeps the format, not the original bytes. The image is decoded and re-encoded; metadata and color handling can change. Your original file on disk is not modified.
