    When you need JPG instead of WebP

    • An upload form, marketplace or print service only accepts JPG or PNG.
    • You are sending a photo by email to someone who uses older software.
    • Your editing program does not open WebP files.

    Transparent images become white

    JPG cannot store transparency. If your WebP has transparent areas, they are filled with white in the JPG. A transparent logo placed on a dark page would show a white box, so use the WebP to PNG converter for those files.

    Things to know

    • Quality is not restored. A lossy WebP that is converted to JPG is compressed again. Use a high quality value to limit further loss.
    • Metadata: camera details and GPS location are not copied to the JPG.
    • Animated WebP: only the first frame is converted.

    Frequently asked questions

    What happens to transparent areas?

    JPG cannot store transparency, so transparent areas become white. If you need transparency, convert to PNG instead.

    Which quality should I choose?

    Use 90 or higher for images you may edit or print, and 80 to 85 for web use or email.

    Why do upload forms ask for JPG?

    JPG is understood by almost every program and device, so many application forms, marketplaces and print services only accept JPG or PNG.

    Are animated WebP files supported?

    Only the first frame is converted, because JPG cannot be animated.
